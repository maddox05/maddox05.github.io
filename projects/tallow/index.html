Tallow

The Story


After spending years building games and infra for kids on Chromebooks, I wanted to build something for the rest of my life - something I'd actually use every week. I'd already been pulling ingredient lists apart in the grocery aisle for years, dodging seed oils, scanning for additives the EU banned a decade ago, and guessing whether the "natural flavors" hid anything weird. Yuka existed, but it scored a Coke higher than raw beef tallow because its model was built around the European nutrient profile, not the stuff seed-oil-skeptics and the carnivore-adjacent crowd actually care about.


So I built Tallow. Point your phone at a barcode and you get a 0-100 score in seconds, with a transparent breakdown of seed oils, additives, NOVA processing level, and plastic exposure. Restaurant map and self-commissioned lab panels for microplastics and PFAS are next.


v1.0 shipped to the App Store in May 2026.

How It Works


Scan a barcode and the app fires a two-tier enrichment pipeline. Tier 1 hits Open Food Facts synchronously - you see the product, ingredient list, nutrition, and a preliminary score within a couple seconds. Tier 2 runs asynchronously in a Supabase Edge Function, calling the OpenAI Responses API with web search to pull contaminant data from oasishealth.app, Florida Department of Health lab reports, EWG, Consumer Reports, FDA testing data, and PubMed studies. The mobile client subscribes to a Supabase Realtime channel on the scan job, so the Sources section populates live - usually ten to fifteen seconds later - without a refresh.


The 0-100 score itself is computed on-device. Four weighted pillars: seed oils, additives (cross-referenced against EU bans and FDA flags), NOVA processing group, and plastic exposure (containers, linings, plus lab-confirmed microplastic and PFAS levels when available). Sourcing signals like organic, grass-fed, pasture-raised, and wild-caught nudge the score up. The math is open - you can audit it.

Features


  • Barcode scanner: Point and shoot. Every product gets a NOVA group (1-4) and a 0-100 Tallow score in seconds, with every flagged additive translated into plain English.
  • Kid-Safe Mode: Stricter thresholds for artificial dyes, controversial preservatives, and stuff banned in EU foods aimed at children.
  • Smart Swaps: When a product flunks, the app suggests a better-scoring alternative in the same category.
  • Lab Tested badge: Surfaces published lab data on the scan card when contaminant studies exist for the product or brand.
  • Save & History: Bookmark products, browse everything you've ever scanned.
  • Restaurant map (coming soon): A crowd-sourced map that scores every restaurant on what they actually cook with - filter by no seed oils, NOVA 1-2, or glass-only packaging.
  • Lab report archive (coming soon): Tallow-commissioned panels on bottled water, takeout containers, and other contact-heavy items, browsable on the web.

Tech Stack


  • Mobile: Expo SDK 54, Expo Router, React Native 0.81, TypeScript. Dev builds via EAS because Apple Sign In and the camera need native modules.
  • Backend: Supabase end-to-end - Postgres, Auth, Realtime, and Edge Functions. No separate API server. The mobile client reads straight through RLS.
  • Data sources: Open Food Facts for the base product catalog; OpenAI Responses API with web search for contaminant enrichment, prompted to prioritize oasishealth.app and Florida Department of Health before other reputable labs.
  • Realtime delivery: Tier 2 enrichment is async, signaled to the client via Supabase Realtime on the scan_jobs table - no polling.
  • Scoring: Standalone TypeScript package (packages/shared) so the scoring lib has its own unit tests and stays portable to the web client.
  • Marketing site: Next.js on Cloudflare Workers via OpenNext - tallow.app.

What's Next


v1.0 nails the core scan-and-score loop on iPhone. The roadmap from here is what turns Tallow into the thing I actually wanted when I started: an Android build, a web client running the same scoring lib, the crowd-sourced restaurant map, and the self-commissioned lab report archive. The hard part isn't the code - it's recruiting a beachhead community of seed-oil-aware users who will actually submit and rate restaurants, because the map is only as good as the contributions behind it.
